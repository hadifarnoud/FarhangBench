# Existing Benchmarks for Persian Language and Iranian Culture

*Survey date: October 2026.* This review covers what already exists, what each benchmark measures, and the gaps FarhangBench is designed to fill. Numbers come from the papers' abstracts and main text. Where a secondary summary was the only source, it is marked *(secondary)*.

---

## 1. Persian-specific cultural benchmarks

| Benchmark | Venue | Size | Format | What it measures | Main finding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PerCul** (Moosavi Monazzah et al.) | NAACL 2025 | 592 | 4-way MCQ over short stories | 11 categories based on Hall's "Triad of Culture" (technical and formal levels): institutions, music, dress, objects, visible behaviour, art, iconic figures, appropriacy, rituals, architecture, food. The cultural concept is implied, not named. | Humans scored about 93%. The best closed model (Claude 3.5 Sonnet) scored 81.7% and the best open model (Llama-3.1-405B) 71.7%. Persian fine-tunes (PersianMind, Dorna) did *worse* than their base models. Translating items to English cost 6.6–14.5 points. Partial-correctness distractors fooled models most often. |
| **TaarofBench** (Gohari Sadr et al.) | EMNLP 2025 | 450 role-play scenarios, 12 topics | Open response, scored by a GPT-4 judge (94% agreement with humans) | Producing *taarof* (ritual politeness): gifts, requests, compliments, payment, seating and doorway precedence, etc. About 70% of scenarios expect taarof and 30% expect directness. | No model scored above 42% on taarof-expected scenarios, against 76–93% on non-taarof ones. Native speakers scored 81.8%, heritage speakers 60%, non-Iranians 42.3%. Persian prompts helped a lot (+32 points for DeepSeek V3). Responses that Western politeness classifiers called polite often violated taarof. Results differed by the user's gender. DPO raised accuracy from 37% to 80%. |
| **PSN – Persian Social Norms** (Saffari et al.) | 2024 | about 1.7k norms | 3-way classification: Expected / Normal / Taboo | Social norms with environment and context labels and English translations. Generated by an LLM, then filtered by humans. | Fine-tuning small encoders helps a lot. Used as a seed for later benchmarks (PerCul appropriacy, TARAZ ISN-SAQ). |
| **TARAZ** (Iranmanesh et al.) | LREC 2026 | 3 datasets | Short answer | A scoring framework that combines Persian morphological normalisation with hybrid syntactic and semantic similarity. | Exact match under-scores Persian. The hybrid metric raised scoring consistency by about 10 points over exact match and agreed better with humans than LLM judges did. |
| **MasalBench** (Kalhor & Bahrak) | arXiv 2026 | 1,000 MCQ + 700 binary | MCQ | Proverbs: (a) a proverb's intent in a dialogue; (b) the English proverb that matches a Persian one. | In-context intent was above 0.90 for all models. Cross-cultural equivalence peaked at 0.79. |
| **FFE-Hallu** | EACL 2026 | 600 | Generation, detection, translation | Hallucination in fixed figurative expressions (idioms and proverbs): generating them from a meaning, spotting fabricated ones, translating EN→FA. | Models often *invent* Persian idioms when translating. GPT-4.1 was best at rejecting fabrications. |
| **GhazalBench** (Kalhor & Yaghoobzadeh) | arXiv 2026 | 200 ghazals (Hafez, Saadi) | Completion and recognition | Access to canonical verse; comparison with Shakespeare's sonnets. | Models understand the meaning but can't reproduce exact verses. Recognition is much better than completion. English sonnets are recalled better. |
| **PersLitEval** | arXiv 2026 | 4,514 | MCQ (from Konkur material) | Literature: spelling, literary devices, grammar, vocabulary, word formation, concepts. | Spelling and word formation are the hardest. Formal linguistic analysis is weaker than conceptual similarity. GPT-4o scored about 50% *(secondary)*. |
| **Persian Literary Creativity** (Tourajmehr et al.) | Findings EMNLP 2025 | 20 topics | Generation, scored by an LLM judge validated with ICC | Torrance-style creativity (originality, fluency, flexibility, elaboration) plus the use of simile, metaphor, hyperbole and antithesis. | Models still misuse literary devices. |
| **PeKA & PK-BETS** (Bourbour Hosseinbeigi et al.) | Findings NAACL 2025 | — | MCQ, short answer, long answer, open-ended | History, literature, cultural knowledge. Designed to limit contamination. | — |
| **FarsEval-PKBETS** (Shamsfard et al.) | arXiv 2025 | 4,000 (3,500 MCQ + 500 short answer) | Mixed | Medicine, law, religion, Persian language, encyclopaedic and social knowledge, ethics, bias, generation. | Average accuracy was below 50%. Short answers were graded by hand with subjective labels, which does not scale. |
| **MELAC** (Momtazi group, PartAI / AUT) | IJCNLP-AACL 2025 | 19 datasets, 41 LLMs | Mostly MCQ | Iranian law, Persian grammar, idioms, Konkur exams, plus localised versions of existing sets. Powers the Open Persian LLM Leaderboard. | Argues for **private** held-out data to resist contamination. |
| **ELAB** (MCILAB / Sharif) | GEM² 2025 | Several sets | Mixed | Alignment in Persian: safety, fairness, social norms (SafeBench-fa, FairBench-fa, SocialBench-fa, GuardBench-fa) plus translated red-teaming sets. | Public alignment leaderboard. |
| **PBBQ** | LREC 2026 | about 37k | BBQ-style MCQ | Social bias in a Persian context, built through human–AI collaboration. | — |

## 2. Persian language and knowledge benchmarks (not cultural in focus)

| Benchmark | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Khayyam Challenge / PersianMMLU** (COLM 2024) | About 20k four-option questions from 38 Iranian school and Konkur exams, with human response rates and difficulty metadata. Original Persian, not translated. |
| **PerCoR** (IJCNLP-AACL 2025) | 106k commonsense sentence-completion items with adversarial distractors (DRESS-AF). Humans scored 89%, o3 92%. **Already saturated.** |
| **ParsiNLU** (2021) | Classic NLU suite: entailment, reading comprehension, QA, sentiment, MT. |
| **PQuAD, ParsQuAD, PersianQA, FarsTail, ArmanEmo** | Extractive QA, NLI and emotion. Pre-LLM era. |
| **Open Persian LLM Leaderboard** (PartAI + AUT) | Part MCQ, ARC-Easy and Challenge (translated), MMLU-Pro (translated), GSM-Persian, AUT MCQ. |
| **MIZAN** (MCINext) | Leaderboard covering understanding, generation, reasoning and knowledge. |
| **APARSIN** (SilkRoadNLP 2026) | Sentiment and MT across 14 Iranic varieties. Performance drops sharply outside standard Persian. |
| **Informal Persian UD treebank, LSCP** | Resources for colloquial (Tehrani) Persian. |

## 3. Multimodal and audio

| Benchmark | Notes |
|---|---|
| **PARSA-Bench** (2026) | 16 audio tasks, including poetry, traditional music and metre (*vazn*) detection. Text baselines beat audio on most tasks, but **audio beats text on poetry**. |
| **Persian in a Court** (EvalMG 2025) | VLMs on Persian-VQA and *Iran-places* (Iranian figures and landmarks). |
| **ParsVQA-Caps** | Persian VQA and captioning with Iranian imagery. |
| **RaahNaameh** (community, HF Space) | 669 frames and 225 video clips of Persian visual culture. Not peer-reviewed. |

## 4. Multilingual and multicultural benchmarks that include Iran or Persian

| Benchmark | Iran / Persian coverage |
|---|---|
| **BLEnD** (NeurIPS 2024) | Iran is one of 16 regions, with Persian and English questions on food, sport, family, education, holidays and work life. Short-answer questions are scored by matching against human answer pools. LLMs do better on cultures with more web presence. |
| **SemEval-2026 Task 7** | Extends BLEnD to 33 language–culture pairs. Reports that exact match under-scores morphologically rich languages such as Persian *(secondary)*. |
| **CulturalBench** | About 1.2k human-written MCQs across 45 regions. A small Iran slice. |
| **NormAd** | 2.6k norm stories across 75 countries. Models handle norm-*violating* stories poorly (sycophancy). |
| **INCLUDE** | Regional exams in 44 languages, including Persian. Models are weaker on *regional* history than on world history. |
| **Global-MMLU** | MMLU in 42 languages, human-verified, with culturally sensitive versus agnostic tags. |
| **World Values Survey (Wave 7, Iran)** | Not a benchmark, but provides real distributions of Iranian opinion for distributional-alignment evaluation. |

## 5. What the field has learned (design lessons)

1. **Translation is not localisation.** Translated MMLU and ARC sets measure Persian reading, not Iranian culture. Native-authored items are needed (PerCul, Khayyam, MELAC).
2. **Multiple choice is saturating and gameable.** PerCoR is already above human level, and MasalBench's in-context task is above 0.90. MCQ needs hard, *culturally* motivated distractors (partial-correctness, neighbouring-culture confusions) and option-permutation checks.
3. **Free-text scoring in Persian is hard.** Exact match under-scores Persian because of orthographic variation (ZWNJ, ی/ي, ک/ك), morphology and synonyms (TARAZ, SemEval-26). Normalisation plus alias lists plus a semantic fallback is the workable recipe.
4. **Pragmatics is the largest gap.** Models know *about* taarof but can't *do* it (TaarofBench, under 42%). Western politeness metrics are the wrong yardstick.
5. **The prompt language matters.** Persian prompts improve pragmatic behaviour (TaarofBench). English translations hurt knowledge recall (PerCul). Benchmarks should report the gap, not hide it.
6. **Implicit cues are harder than explicit ones.** Removing "in Iran" hurts smaller models (TaarofBench), and story-embedded concepts are harder than direct questions (PerCul).
7. **Recall of canonical text is weak** even when meaning is understood (GhazalBench). Models also **fabricate** idioms and verses (FFE-Hallu).
8. **Persian fine-tunes can lose cultural competence** (PerCul: Dorna and PersianMind). The benchmark must not reward fluency alone.
9. **Contamination is a live risk.** Public Persian benchmarks get scraped, so MELAC argues for private test sets.
10. **Tehran-centrism.** Almost all data comes from university students in big cities (an acknowledged limitation in PerCul). Ethnic, regional, religious-minority, generational and diaspora perspectives are largely missing, and so is the wider Persianate world (Dari, Tajik).

## 6. Gaps FarhangBench targets

| Gap | Existing coverage | FarhangBench response |
|---|---|---|
| Persian **production quality**: orthography (نیم‌فاصله), register, naturalness versus translationese | Almost none | Track A (Language), with automatic orthography metrics |
| **Formulaic and pragmatic competence** beyond taarof: honorific verbs, ritual exchange formulas, implicature | TaarofBench (taarof only) | Track D (Adab & Pragmatics) |
| **Poetry as practice**: prosody (عروض), qafiye and radif, attribution, *fake-verse detection* | GhazalBench (recall only), PARSA (audio metre) | Track B (Adabiyat) |
| **Practical everyday competence**: Jalali calendar arithmetic, toman versus rial, Iranian formats and institutions | Scattered | Track C (Everyday Life) |
| **Regional, ethnic and religious diversity**; Persianate world | Almost none | Coverage tags plus quotas in every track, and a Varieties task |
| **Misconceptions and stereotypes** about Iran (for example "Iranians are Arabs") | None | Track E (Values & Fairness) |
| **Hallucination and abstention**: fabricated proverbs, verses, people | FFE-Hallu (idioms only) | A cross-cutting *Fabrication probe* axis |
| **Robustness reporting**: FA vs EN vs Finglish; explicit vs implicit cues | Partial | Mandatory parallel variants and reported *gap metrics* |
| **Contamination resistance** | MELAC (private sets) | Hidden test split, canary string, dated "living" refresh |
